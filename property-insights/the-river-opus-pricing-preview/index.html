The River Opus pricing preview: the 2-bedroom next door already costs $2.70M

No prices are out for The River Opus yet. So I looked at what buyers have actually paid for the homes around it, bedroom for bedroom.

The short versionOverview of The River OpusThe locationNo land price, so look at the land around itWhat the new launches nearby sold forWhat resale homes nearby sold forMy takeHow I worked this out

By Justin Ngai · Pricing preview · 11 November 2026

The River Opus is the redevelopment of Valley Point and Fraser Suites Singapore on River Valley Road, by Frasers Property and Mitsubishi Estate. The plan is about 407 homes on 999-year land, with the launch planned for 2027.

It will not launch into an empty market. Since August 2025, four 99-year projects around Great World MRT have sold about 2,000 homes between them, and a fifth site was awarded in June 2026.

Because there is no price list, the useful question for now is a simple one. What does the same number of bedrooms cost in the homes around it, new and resale?

The short version

  • A resale 2-bedroom at Valley Park, the 999-year condo next door, sold at a median of $2.70M over the past 12 months.
  • New 2-bedrooms at the 99-year launches near Great World sold at $1.81M to $2.03M. At The Robertson Opus, the one new 999-year launch nearby, they sold at $2.46M.
  • Land beside Great World went from $1,202 to $1,730 per square foot of floor area in 26 months.
  • The River Opus has no land bid to work from, because the site was not bought at a government tender.

The location

The site is on River Valley Road, a short distance west of Great World. Great World MRT on the Thomson-East Coast Line is the nearest station, about 610 m from its closest exit. Tiong Bahru on the East West Line is about 760 m away across the water, and Havelock about 850 m. Great World mall is about 540 m away, and River Valley Primary School about 970 m.

So this is the western end of River Valley Road. The four 99-year launches below sit closer to Great World MRT.

Straight-line distances measured on OneMap. Walking routes are longer.

No land price, so look at the land around it

With a government land sale, I start from what the developer paid for the land, because that sets the floor for the launch price. The River Opus is a redevelopment of two existing buildings, so there is no tender price to start from.

What we do have is five government sites sold near Great World since 2024.

The newest site cost 44% more than the first one, which was sold 26 months earlier. All five are 99-year leasehold.

I think this matters for The River Opus. The next 99-year launch beside Great World starts from a much higher land cost than the four selling now. That is the market The River Opus will launch into.

Valley Park is the closest comparison on tenure. It sits next to the site and is also 999-year. Its 2-bedrooms changed hands at a median of $2.70M in the past 12 months. That is more than a new 2-bedroom at any of the five launches above.

Valley Park’s prices have moved slowly. Across all its resales, the median went from $2,048 psf in 2022 to $2,232 psf in 2026, about 9% in four years.

Median resale price, December 2025 to November 2026, from URA caveats.

My take

I think three numbers frame The River Opus before any price list comes out.

$2.70M is what a 2-bedroom on 999-year land already costs next door, in an existing building.

$2.46M is what buyers paid for a new 999-year 2-bedroom at The Robertson Opus, from the same lead developer.

$1,730 is the land price for the next 99-year site beside Great World, 44% above where Zyon Grand’s land started.

So when the prices come out, I would put the 2-bedroom and 3-bedroom prices against these, bedroom for bedroom, before looking at anything else. How I worked this out

  • Prices are from URA caveats, up to November 2026.
  • URA does not record bedrooms, so I matched each sale’s floor area to the project’s unit types from its floor plans and listings.
  • A box says “Too few sales” where fewer than three sales matched.
  • This compares past sales. It is not a forecast of The River Opus’s prices.
