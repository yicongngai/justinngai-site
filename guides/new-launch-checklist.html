New launch or resale condo: a comparison checklist

When comparing launches, I would start with a specific unit and its total price. Then check what else you can buy with the same budget.

In this guide

By Justin Ngai · Published guide · 2 October 2026 · Revised 22 October 2026

Decide what you need from the home

Write down where you would live and how much space you need. Include the monthly payment you are comfortable with and when you need to move. Keep those requirements beside you when looking at each project.

Check the total price and room sizes

The psf figure is useful, but you still need to know the total price. Check bedroom dimensions on a scaled plan and see where your furniture would fit. Look at how much of the unit is taken up by corridors and balconies.

Check the stack and surroundings

Check the facing and floor of the unit you are considering. Visit the roads and buildings around the site at different times of day. Ask whether a view is protected and what future development could change it.

Compare nearby resale homes too

I would compare nearby resale homes with the same bedroom count and useful functions, rather than match floor area alone. Check their age and condition, then allow for renovation and maintenance. Compare the total purchase price alongside what each home lets your household do.

Budget for the time before completion

Add stamp duty and legal fees to your budget. Work out the financing costs and what you will spend on housing before completion, then allow for maintenance and renovation. Check the dated completion information against the contract. Your lender should confirm the loan position before you commit.

Check whether you can hold the home longer

Ask what happens if you need to hold the home longer than planned or your income changes. Decide how much cash you would need in those situations. I would use the maximum loan as a limit to check, then choose a monthly payment you are comfortable sustaining.

A new-launch versus resale comparison sheet.

Start with two homes that solve the same bedroom and daily-use needs. Different floor areas can still support the same household; similar floor areas can have very different room arrangements.

For an upcoming option such as One Chuan Grove at Lorong Chuan, begin with the confirmed project facts and leave unreleased unit details open. Choose a completed alternative you could actually consider, then record both sides using the prompts below.

These are research prompts, not price quotations or a recommendation to buy either option. A missing price or completion detail stays unresolved until it is confirmed; do not fill it with a return forecast.

Keep the evidence beside the comparison

Use URA’s private residential transaction service for recorded sales, and IRAS’s Buyer’s Stamp Duty guidance when building the purchase budget. Have your lender and lawyer confirm the figures and conditions for your own purchase.
