HDB to condo: plan eligibility, cash flow & timing

Before deciding whether to sell or buy first, work out what you can use from the sale of your flat and when those funds will be available.

In this guide

By Justin Ngai · Published guide · 2 October 2026

Check your flat’s eligibility first

HDB requires owners and spouses to fulfil the applicable minimum occupation period before acquiring private residential property. Standard flats generally have a five-year MOP; Plus and Prime flats have ten years. Check your own flat’s conditions and eligibility directly with HDB.

Calculate the proceeds you can use

Start with the expected sale price, then deduct the outstanding loan, required CPF refund and sale costs. Keep the resulting cash and CPF amounts separate. Check when you can use each amount for the next purchase.

Compare selling first and buying first

Selling first gives you a clearer figure for the funds available. You may need somewhere to stay before the next home is ready. Buying first lets you secure a home, but you need to fund the purchase while still owning your flat. Put both sequences on a dated cash-flow plan before choosing.

Check whether you qualify for ABSD remission

Some married couples who buy a second home jointly can qualify for ABSD remission. IRAS requires at least one spouse to be a Singapore Citizen, along with other conditions and a deadline for selling the first home. Ask your lawyer to confirm whether you qualify and which deadline applies before exercising the option.

Plan the gap between homes

Put the sale and purchase completion dates on the same calendar. Add renovation and moving time. If you need an extension of stay, discuss it early and check the applicable rules. Also decide where you would stay if the dates change.

Resolve the questions before committing

Keep the expected proceeds and confirmed loan amount together with the purchase costs. Record what still needs checking and who can answer it, such as HDB, your banker, CPF Board or your lawyer. Get those answers before committing to the purchase.
