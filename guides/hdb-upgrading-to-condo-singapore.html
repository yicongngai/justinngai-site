HDB to condo: plan eligibility, cash flow & timing

Before deciding whether to sell or buy first, work out what you can use from the sale of your flat, what the bank will lend and when the stamp duty falls due.

By Justin Ngai · Published guide · 2 October 2026 · Revised 26 November 2026

Check your flat’s MOP first

You cannot buy private property while your flat is still within its Minimum Occupation Period. That is five years for most flats and ten years for Plus and Prime flats, counted from key collection, or from completion if you bought the flat on the resale market. Check the date for your own flat with HDB.

Work out the cash and CPF you will have

From the sale price, take off the outstanding HDB or bank loan. Then the CPF you used for the flat, plus 2.5% a year accrued interest, goes back into your CPF Ordinary Account, where you can use it again for the condo. What is left after that is cash. Keep the two amounts separate, because part of the down payment on a condo must be cash: at least 5% of the price on a first housing loan, or 25% if you still have another housing loan.

Check what the bank will lend

For a first housing loan, banks can lend up to 75% of the price, if the loan term is 30 years or less and ends by age 65. All your monthly debt repayments, the new mortgage included, cannot go above 55% of your gross monthly income. If your HDB loan is still running when you buy, the limit for the condo loan drops to 45%.

Compare selling first and buying first

Selling first gives you a firm figure for the funds you have, and you pay no ABSD. You may need somewhere to stay until the condo is ready. Buying first lets you secure the home you want and move only once, but you pay 20% ABSD up front and borrow less while the flat is unsold. The table below puts the two side by side.

Check whether you qualify for the ABSD refund

A Singapore Citizen who buys a condo while still owning a flat pays 20% ABSD within 14 days. A married couple buying jointly, with at least one Singapore Citizen, can get it back if the flat is sold within six months: of the purchase date for a completed condo, or of the TOP or CSC, whichever comes first, for a new launch. If neither of you is a Singapore Citizen, there is no refund, and HDB requires a PR owner to sell the flat within six months of buying private property. Ask your lawyer to confirm your case before exercising the option.

Plan the gap between homes

Put the sale and purchase completion dates on one calendar, with renovation and moving time. If you sell first, you can ask the buyer of your flat for an extension of stay of up to three months, then rent or stay with family if you need longer. If you buy a new launch first, the six-month clock for the ABSD refund starts at TOP, which gives you time to sell, but the condo payments start while it is being built.

Resolve the questions before committing

Keep the expected proceeds and confirmed loan amount together with the purchase costs. Record what still needs checking and who can answer it, such as HDB, your banker, CPF Board or your lawyer. Get those answers before committing to the purchase.

Buy a resale condo first, then sell the flat inside the option period

For a resale condo there is a middle route. Take a long option on the condo, sell the flat within it, and have the flat’s option exercised on or before the day you exercise the condo’s. The flat is then treated as sold when you buy, so no ABSD is payable. If the flat’s sale slips past that day, the 20% ABSD applies and you are back to the refund route above.

Every date moves with your own option period, completion period and HDB timeline. Ask your lawyer to confirm the dates before you sign.

If the condo is a new launch: when the money is due.

You do not pay the whole price up front. After the first 20%, the bank loan is drawn down in stages as the building goes up, and interest is charged only on what has been drawn. A quarter of the price falls due at TOP, which is also when the six-month clock for an ABSD refund starts.
